General notes

About

I lead non‑contact measurement and smart sensor development at Mitutoyo's mechatronics R&D section, with a filed patent (DE102024129207A1) on a dual‑sensor lens measurement system. Recent focus has expanded into physical AI — VLA models, robot arms, and simulation — alongside my core work in electromagnetic sensor design and motor control.

Day to day, I run Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, Google Antigravity, and Cursor as working tools, not novelties — they've cut my COMSOL–MATLAB–Python simulation iteration time from days to hours. I studied sliding‑mode control for drone attitude stabilization for my M.Eng at Tokyo Metropolitan University (MEXT Scholar), and I still keep one foot in the hobby side: astronomy, dinosaurs, drones, and small game‑dev projects on the side.

Based in Tokyo, Japan. Working languages: Indonesian (native), English (C1), Japanese (JLPT N3).

Selected work

Four items that best describe what I actually do: instruments, simulation pipelines, control systems, and the tooling around them.

001

WLO non‑contact lens measurement system

Dual SIM sensor instrument with an XY magnetic linear motor stage and a decenter calibration algorithm, reaching 0.8–1.0 µm accuracy across three delivered prototypes. Filed as patent DE102024129207A1.
Vision systems · EM actuation · Control algorithms · Calibration
002

Smart eddy‑current sensor (ECS) — new business development

Drove a smart sensor concept from ideation to a working planar ECS prototype for gap and thread‑surface evaluation, as a selected member of Mitutoyo's New Business Strategic Development Office. Joint R&D with Mitutoyo Research & Development America.
Sensor design · Altium / KiCAD · Prototyping · Feasibility
003

COMSOL → MATLAB → Python simulation pipeline

End‑to‑end electromagnetic simulation tooling built around COMSOL 6.4 with MATLAB LiveLink and a Python GUI. Refactored undocumented legacy scripts into a maintained tool with a proper results/plot module — parametric sweep iteration went from days to hours.
COMSOL 6.4 · MATLAB LiveLink · Python · PySide6
004

Quadcopter sliding‑mode control with chattering attenuation

Master's research at Tokyo Metropolitan University: a trajectory generation design that attenuates chattering in sliding‑mode attitude control for quadcopters. Presented at the SICE Annual Conference 2023.
Sliding‑mode control · MATLAB/Simulink · UAV dynamics
